## What looked useful

Across 12 seeds x 5,000 calls, evidence_ledger achieved 100% mean/min safe pass and 100% mean/min unsafe block, compared with static_guard at 85.92% mean safe pass and 65.88% mean unsafe block. Median evidence-ledger latency was 18.07 us/call. Hash-chain verification accepted the untampered ledger and rejected modified, deleted, and reordered variants.

## Boundaries and scale limits

Synthetic workload only; dangerous effects were dry-run simulated; evidence labels were generated by the benchmark rather than real users; no real LLM, prompt-injection suite, malicious tool server, or production agent framework was tested.

## Claim scope

In a deterministic synthetic local-tool-call benchmark, a tiny hash-chained evidence ledger with policy-required evidence fields blocked all generated missing-evidence unsafe calls, preserved all generated approved benign and sensitive calls, and detected simple ledger tampering with low absolute Python overhead.

## Why it stopped

Closed as no-paper useful signal because the evidence is synthetic/proxy-only and supports the mechanism but not a publication-grade real-agent safety claim.

## Recommended next action

Run a bounded follow-up that wraps a real local agent/tool protocol and replays prompt-injection tasks with real proposed tool calls, while preserving the same safe-pass, unsafe-block, tamper-detection, and overhead metrics.
