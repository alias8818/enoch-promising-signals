## What looked useful

A ledger that stores command outputs with hashes and requires claim-level evidence references can enforce a narrow but real claim-checking contract on CPU command runs: 0/7 unsupported claims allowed versus 7/7 for an unconstrained baseline, 0/9 supported claims falsely rejected, and tampered evidence rejected by hash mismatch.

## Boundaries and scale limits

Small local task suite; claims were harness-authored rather than produced by independent LLM agents; predicates were structured and simple; no long autonomous sessions, blind human labels, semantic entailment, or multi-agent corpus validation.

## Claim scope

In a controlled Tier 1 local CPU-agent harness with six real shell/Python commands and sixteen known-ground-truth final-report claims, ledger-enforced evidence references plus predicate checks rejected all seeded unsupported claims, accepted all supported claims, and detected post-hoc evidence tampering.

## Why it stopped

Tier 1 mechanism evidence met the controlled threshold, but it is no-paper useful signal because the claims were controlled and structured rather than generated by independent real agents.

## Recommended next action

Run a bounded deepen test on real LLM CPU-agent transcripts: collect at least 10 short autonomous command sessions, label final claims blind against ledger evidence, and compare unsupported-claim rate with and without ledger enforcement.
