## What looked useful

Across eight synthetic seeds, the composite detector achieved mean ROC-AUC 0.9968 and mean recall 0.9859 at mean FPR 0.0526, outperforming loss-only ROC-AUC mean 0.9430. The main failure mode was over-flagging strong honest trainees, with mean FPR 0.1061.

## Boundaries and scale limits

No real volunteer data, no natural-language model scoring, no adversarial adaptation, and no operational adjudication were tested. Synthetic sessions were generated by hand-coded distributions with 6000 sessions per seed across eight seeds.

## Claim scope

Synthetic volunteer-training sessions only: a transparent composite detector can identify simulated answer-key, collusion, and adaptive-lookup cheating better than challenge loss or accuracy alone.

## Why it stopped

Synthetic proxy supports the mechanism but is not direct/full validation; false-positive risk for strong honest trainees and missing real replay evidence prevent paper-ready closure.

## Recommended next action

Run a bounded blinded replay with human-simulated or real volunteer logs; do not write a paper from the synthetic-only result.
