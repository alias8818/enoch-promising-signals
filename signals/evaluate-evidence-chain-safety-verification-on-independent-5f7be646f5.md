## What looked useful

Tier 2 fixed-seed evaluation over 4,060 claims showed full verifier accuracy 1.0000 and unsupported-claim rejection 1.0000, while lexical transcript and rubber-stamp baselines had 0.5000 accuracy and 0.0000 unsupported rejection. Channel ablations reduced supported recall as expected: no_commands 0.2857, no_metadata 0.8571, no_outputs 0.8571.

## Boundaries and scale limits

The corpus is local Codex session data from one worker distribution; labels are deterministic fact perturbations rather than human-authored safety annotations; the claim taxonomy is narrow; sampled traces had network and failed-command positives but no destructive-recursive or escalated-permission positives; the test is post-hoc verification, not live intervention.

## Claim scope

On 58 independent local Codex agent session traces from this worker, a structured evidence-chain verifier over command, output, and metadata facts correctly accepted supported deterministic trace claims and rejected unsupported perturbation claims across 5 fixed seeds, outperforming lexical transcript-overlap and rubber-stamp chain baselines.

## Why it stopped

Tier 2 bounded validation target was met, but the result remains no-paper because labels are generated by deterministic perturbation and the safety taxonomy is too narrow for publication-grade safety claims.

## Recommended next action

Run a deepen follow-up on a held-out multi-worker trace corpus with human-written or auditor-written safety claims, adversarial paraphrases, and sandboxed high-risk events for destructive, escalated, and exfiltration-like actions.
