## What looked useful

Caching is useful inside the corpus verifier: target-only cache was 1.83x faster than no-cache, and speculative cache was 1.49x-1.53x faster than speculative no-cache while scanning about 71% fewer corpus positions. The combined speculative approach failed the practical baseline test: cached gamma-4 and gamma-8 speculative decoding reached only 0.644x and 0.567x of cached target-only tokens/sec.

## Boundaries and scale limits

No LLM target, no GPU batched forward pass, no tokenizer-level language model, and no production suffix-array/SIMD verifier were tested. The result is a medium local mechanism ablation, not publication-grade broad validation.

## Claim scope

On a 1.2 MB public-text byte-level corpus with an order-5 corpus-backed n-gram target verifier, order-3 n-gram draft, five fixed seeds, and 8192 generated bytes per variant, verifier caching reduces corpus lookup work and speeds speculative variants versus no-cache ablations, but cached speculative decoding is slower than a cached target-only baseline in actual wall-clock tokens/sec.

## Why it stopped

Medium fixed-seed local evidence supports the cache mechanism but directly fails the real cached target-only wall-clock baseline for this implementation.

## Recommended next action

Stop this run as no-paper useful signal; the next bounded test, if pursued, should implement a genuinely block-parallel corpus verifier and require wall-clock speedup over cached target-only, not just fewer corpus scans.
