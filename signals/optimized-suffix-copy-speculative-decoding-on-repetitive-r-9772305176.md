## What looked useful

Online suffix-copy drafting produced persistent exact-match acceptance on real Gutenberg and RFC corpora, with stronger ideal call reductions on the more repetitive RFC texts; practical wall-clock value remains unproven.

## Boundaries and scale limits

No neural target model, BPE tokenizer, KV-cache integration, GPU latency, or production serving path was tested. Long-draft settings also increase verifier positions per generated byte by about 13x to 18x in the best ideal-call rows.

## Claim scope

Tier 1 byte-level, prefix-only simulation on four real repetitive corpora shows suffix-copy drafting can reduce ideal serial verifier calls by about 2.1x to 3.1x versus one-token autoregressive decoding.

## Why it stopped

Closed as no-paper useful signal: the Tier 1 direct simulation supports the mechanism but does not validate neural-model wall-clock speedup or publication-grade systems benefit.

## Recommended next action

Run a bounded GPT-2-small token-level latency experiment on the same corpora comparing no drafting, suffix-copy drafting, and short-draft variants with exact-output verification.
